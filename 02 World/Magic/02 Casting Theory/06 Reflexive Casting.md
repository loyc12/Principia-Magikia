**Reflexive Casting** ( sometimes called *cantrip casting* ) is the act of funnelling raw mana into a spell construct which has been thoroughly memorised in the caster's mind beforehand. Said construct declines and processes the supplied mana into the desired effect, so long as the targeted reagent falls within its acceptable predefined range. Otherwise, it remains inert, simply letting the mana pass through itself.

In sum, Reflexive casting is to Structured Casting what parroting sentences is to producing original speech. As such, reflexive casters do not meaningfully alter a spell construct, but simply activate a spell construct they generally do not fully understand, and which originates from the handed-down work of a more structured casting magi, which would have initially designed the spell construct.

Minor variations of commonly found **cantrip constructs** are frequent, and there is sometimes multiple different families of these cantrips with near identical end effect, the foremost of which are the multiple variants of heating and lighting cantrips, which also happen to be the two most widespread cantrips.

Reflexive casting is the method most magi novices default to using, since it is comparatively simple and generally reliable, as the user only need minimal understanding of the  underlying magic principles to use it ( notably control of mana and spell construct memorisation ). It is the most widely practised form of magic, and most people at least know a few cantrips by the time they turn 12 seasons, although this fact is most prevalent in cities and larger towns.

## Strengths :

- commonly taught
- reliable after training
- does not require deep theory
- useful for daily cantrips

## Weaknesses :

- limited power
- limited flexibility
- mental fatigue
- construct degradation/interference
- impossible to trivially combine effects

## Limitations :

- **Low parameter range**  
    A person can make a light brighter or dimmer, but cannot redesign the spell into a laser, illusion, or heat lamp.
- **Poor composability**  
    Two cantrips cannot trivially be combined. A person who knows Heating and Launch cantrips wound find it impossible to produce a flying fireball without breaking down both cantrips and rebuilding a new one, which enters the domain of Structured casting.
- **Memory burden**  
    Each cantrip occupies trained mental “shape.” Learning too many produces interference, confusion, instability, or reduced reliability. An averaged, trained reflexive caster will generally know no more than 24 cantrips at a time.
- **Pattern drift**  
    Poorly practised cantrips slowly degrade. This creates folk variants, regional dialects of magic, suboptimal construct forms and, in worse cases dangerous malformed spells.
- **Safety by overconstraint**  
    Common cantrips are weak not because weak magic is all ordinary people can do, but because public versions are deliberately over-limited to prevent accidents, especially in what is an accepted reagent to target, or with various flow limiting conditions.
