**Wild Casting** ( sometimes referred to as *instinctive casting* ) is the act of casting a spell without the use of a symbolic spell construct. It is generally highly mana inefficient, and thus can only serve to create basic effects. Wild casting is most often seen in creatures, but some plants are also documented as being able to cast mana. There are also rumours of certain members of specific [[sapient races]] ( primarily children ) being able to do wild casting, but these remain unconfirmed.

The leading theory behind wild casting is the existence of dedicated organs, which fulfil the purpose of mana constructs, along with an instinctive mental ability to funnel mana into this pseudo-construct organ. Dissection of these suspected organs have found no symbolic patterns, excluding their use in wild casting.

As such, wild casting would only be possible for beings endowed with these body parts, and the instinctual understanding of mana manipulation. This would explain why magical creatures may be able to cast simple spells despite lacking sapience, or sometimes even sentience altogether.

## Strengths :

- incredibly fast at casting
- can be performed under stress
- integrated with anatomy

## Weaknesses :

- narrow effect range
- impossible to alter its outcome
- inefficient outside evolved use-case
