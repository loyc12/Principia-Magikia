# Sapient Species

Species should be designed from biology and history rather than from a single cultural archetype. Their sensory range, body plan, lifespan, development, metabolism, and mana interaction should create practical consequences for architecture, work, travel, kinship, medicine, and political participation.

Those consequences must not turn a species into a culture. Arrival history, migration, mixed communities, class, occupation, and local institutions all matter at least as much as physiology.

The most common species of sapient might ultimately be native to the expanse, but that is yet to be decided.

## Social and Communicative Constraints

The species relevant to the early story should be different enough that interspecies life requires adaptation, but compatible enough for ordinary companionship, cooperation, and shared institutions to be credible. Most can produce and hear a substantial common range of simple sounds, though not every individual can become fluent in every other species' language.

This permits local trade languages and creoles built around a shared phonology, while leaving native languages, registers, and some concepts partly inaccessible across species. The specific linguistic consequences belong in [[Languages|Languages]].

## Questions for Later Species Design

- What ecological or Arrival history explains this population's presence in the local region?
- Which biological differences impose recurring practical costs or advantages?
- Which adaptations are visible in shared spaces, and which are private or institutionally accommodated?
- Which communicative limits matter in intimate conversation, education, law, or magical instruction?
