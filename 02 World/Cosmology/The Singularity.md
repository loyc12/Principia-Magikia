# The Singularity

The **Singularity** is the luminous body at the centre of [[The Expanse|the Expanse]]. It provides the world with illumination and is the only known site at which Spent mana is Primed back into its raw, reactive state. To inhabitants of the Expanse, it serves the practical role that suns or skylights serve in sourceworlds, though its nature and behaviour are unlike either.

## Seasonal Motion

The Singularity oscillates north and south by about { 315 km }. This motion is thought to cause the seasonal variation between the two hemispheres, which complete a cycle every { 19587 hours ( roughly 816 earth days )}.

There are two generally recognised seasons, Winter and Summer. Their transitions occur when the Singularity is exactly at the centre of the Expanse and shadows are vertical. Each season reaches its apex when the Singularity is at its furthest point along its axis of travel, leaving diagonal shadows.

This cycle leaves the distant poles dry and subject to large seasonal temperature shifts, while the equator remains at a mostly steady temperature. The equator nevertheless experiences the strongest seasonal storms, as air and water move between the hemispheres most intensely near each seasonal apex. The resulting contact between cool, dry air and cooler waters from one hemisphere and the warm, humid air of the other brings heavy winds and rainfall.

Together, these conditions make the equator and poles poorly suited to permanent settlement. Most settled societies therefore occupy the two bands between those extremes.

## Illumination and Night

The Singularity cycles between illuminated and dark states every { 25.6 hours }. It remains dark for { 8.9 hours }, changes state at a steady linear rate over { 1.3 hours }, and provides { 14.1 hours } of full illumination.

At night, luminous specks of varying size rise from near the Expanse's surface towards the Singularity. They brighten as they ascend but remain dim by the standards of most sourceworld nightskies. The Singularity itself is surrounded by a dim, turbulent cloud of lights, often compared colloquially to a swarm of fireflies or a starry nebula.
