# Mana Flow

Mana is a field of discrete particles, each one being in one of **3 possible states** :

- **Primed** ( charged with **unattuned potential** )
- **Attuned** ( charged with **attuned potential**, differentiated along multiple **mana attributes** )
- **Spent** ( uncharged, as the potential was consumed during permutation )

Collectively, Primed, Spent, and the various attuned mana attributes are known as **mana types**, and the process of converting from one of these to another is known as **mana permutation**. Permutation invariably follow one of these **3 permutation processes** : 

| Permutation      | Process Formula                                                                   |
| ---------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Attunement**\* | Primed => Attuned( attribute ) x ( 1 - Ratio ) + Spent x Ratio + Permutation Loss |
| **Expenditure**  | Attuned( attribute ) => Spent + Operations( attribute )                           |
| **Priming**\*\*  | Spent + Singularity => Primed                                                     |
*\*Attunement releases residual physical energy and disordered mana-field disturbances, collectively termed permutation loss.*
\*\**Priming occurs exclusively around the singularity ( the light source at the centre of the expense )*

These mana types all interact amongst themselves and each other through repellent forces, pushing mana away from highly concentrated areas towards less concentrated ones.

They are also all affected by gravitational pull, with Spent mana being pulled upwards ( where it eventually gets permuted back into Primed mana ), while the other states are all pulled downwards at varying rates, pooling in low-lying areas of the surface.

Different materials have various interactive properties with some or all of the mana types, resulting in many biological, metallic, crystalline matter attracting or repelling mana to various degrees.

Of all the mana types, Primed mana is the most reactive both with itself, other mana types, and mana-interactive matter, while Spent mana is the least, being almost completely inert. All other type's properties generally lie in between these two extremes.

**Flow Theory** is the field of study which pertains to the different ways mana is pushed and pulled with these various forces.

# Mana Attunement

**Mana potential** is the capacity of a mana particle to perform an attributed operation. Physical energy may be created, redistributed, consumed, or conserved depending on the attribute involved.

**Attunement** is the process of **converting Primed mana into** **Attuned Mana**. This process occurs naturally, at a rate dependent on the relative local concentration of the target Attuned mana compared to the local concentration of Primed mana and, to a lesser degree, Spent mana.

The greatest rates of this **Background Attunement** are found in locales with high Primed mana concentration, and low Attuned and Spent mana concentrations. Existing Attuned mana biases nearby Primed mana toward the same attribute, making attunement partially self-propagating, but with diminishing rates due to rising attuned concentration.

This conversion process also generates an amount of Spent mana, called **Waste mana** in this context. The **Waste mana ratio** ( the proportion of used Primed mana which becomes Spent mana ) depends on both the specific attribute at play, as well as the attunement process itself, with faster processes generally being less efficient than slower ones.

Attuning attributes can also be done directly by a caster ( and is often one of the first exercises novice magi tend to be taught ), but doing so is rarely efficient in both time, effort, and purity of results. As such, There exists a wide range of mana construct dedicated to automating the attunement of Primed mana into the various attributes.

# Mana Constructs

Mana constructs are the building blocks of spells, as they allow for the conversion of Attuned mana into the specific desired effect, and can have said effect be nudged by predefined conditions, allowing for conditional or self-regulating spells.

There are **5 essential phases** to **creating and triggering** a functioning mana construct :

- **Construction** ( **create** a stable **construct scaffold** out of Primed mana )
- **Declaration** ( **imbue** the construct with the desired **conditions** and **parameters** )
- **Activation** ( **seal** the construct and **enable** its future execution )
- **Execution** ( **enact** the construct's function based on its declaration )
- **Termination** ( gracefully **dissipate** the construct without perturbing surrounding constructs )

Oftentimes, these phases can overlap ( for example, construction and declaration or execution and termination ), but they are invariably present, even if they are not always readily visible.

|Magic phase|Computing analogy|
|---|---|
|Construction|Allocate hardware/runtime environment|
|Declaration|Define program and parameters|
|Activation|Compile, validate, seal, arm|
|Execution|Run and supervise|
|Termination|Deallocate, flush, and fail safely|

Below is a more detailed breakdown of each phase :

### Construction

It is necessary for a mana construct to be contained within an insulating shell, known as a **construct scaffold**. Theses scaffolds are built out of Primed mana, which has the ability to form and maintain certain shapes when properly manipulated. These shapes sometimes occur naturally in location with a high density of Primed mana, but these occurrences lack the size, organisation, or structure to be proper construct scaffolds. 

Primed mana incorporated into a sealed scaffold is geometrically constrained and cannot readily undergo attunement. It retains potential but is functionally isolated from ordinary flow. Scaffold damage releases that Primed mana, often causing uncontrolled local attunement or repulsive discharge.

Scaffolds are generally built in intricate patterns of lines connected by their ends. The simplest of these patterns are the edges of all 5 platonic solids, but more complex mana constructs often require more intricate shapes.

**Scaffold Engineering** is the field of study which concerns itself with the shaping of construct scaffolds, the density and stability of their distinct configurations, and the ways to manipulate Primed mana into constructing them.

### Declaration

An empty construct scaffold cannot, by itself, enact any magical effect, other than disrupt nearby constructs if improperly disassembled. As such, it is necessary to **imbue** these scaffold with **construct components**, which embed specific functions inside the construct, defining its global behaviour once executed.

This imbuing process is known as **component declaration**, and the mana attribute(s) used therein decides which operation the construct can perform onto the reagent. These component may occur any number of times inside a construct, and are linked together and to the outside world based on their internal structure as well as the general scaffold architecture. 

Common components are described as **component archetypes** that can readily be used to instantiate **component variants**, saving magi from having to manually define each component for every new construct they create.

### Termination

A properly designed construct will have a way to handle all likely **termination cases** (which is to say the different ways the execution can finish, either purposefully or accidentally ) so as to avoid collapse of the construct, which can lead to heavy collateral damages to surrounding constructs and the spell as a whole.

Proper termination is known as **graceful termination**, while improper termination is referred to as **erroneous termination**. Avoiding erroneous termination should be one of the top concerns of any spell designer, as a spell might otherwise cause catastrophic damage, including the loss of life.

Below is a list of frequent construct failure types termination would need to handle :

| Failure Type             | Description                                                     |
| ------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Source starvation**    | Insufficient or interrupted mana supply                         |
| **Attribute mismatch**   | Supplied mana does not match component requirements             |
| **Selection failure**    | Reagent cannot be resolved                                      |
| **Boundary leak**        | Effect escapes its declared region                              |
| **Regulation failure**   | Output exceeds declared or safe values                          |
| **Feedback oscillation** | Corrective loops repeatedly overcompensate                      |
| **Scaffold fracture**    | Construct topology loses containment                            |
| **Component deadlock**   | Components wait indefinitely on one another                     |
| **Runaway execution**    | Termination condition becomes unreachable                       |
| **Dissipation overflow** | Waste or excess mana cannot be safely discharged                |
| **Link cascade**         | Failure propagates through linked constructs                    |
| **Semantic mismatch**    | Declaration is valid but does not do what the designer intended |

# Spell Composition

At their core, spells are/represent an **operationally unified** system of one or more mana constructs, connected through declared interfaces and directed toward a shared effect, process, or termination condition.

In sum, they are/represent **sets of mana constructs** that interact to perform **composite operations** upon one or more **reagents**.

While it is technically possible to accomplish any spell effect with a single mana construct, said mana construct would rapidly become too large and complex, and it is thus preferable to **compose spells** by interconnecting more self-contained mana constructs, each with a dedicated purpose or task.

There is many ways to represent spells, but all converge on a specific notion : that of **glyphs**, **abstract representation** of various spell components, scaffolds, and other basic elements. 

The only requirements for a valid glyph system is for them to be reproducible, differentiable to each other, and composable into **spell formulas**. While not necessarily visual, glyph systems often tend to be based on symbols or text, speech, or music, but other **abstract patterns** can be used.

Colloquially, the term "spell" may refer to 5 distinct but interconnect concepts :

| Term                | Definition                                                                     |
| ------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Spell Formula**   | The specification of a spell encoded in a arbitrary glyph system               |
| **Spell Template**  | The notation-independent logical design represented by one or more formulas    |
| **Spell Assembly**  | The network of mana constructs that constitutes a composite spell              |
| **Spell Execution** | A particular activation and runtime state of a spell assembly                  |
| **Spell Results**   | Resulting magical operations of spell execution and their natural consequences |


**NOTES :**

| Term      | Computing analogy              |
| --------- | ------------------------------ |
| Formula   | Source representation          |
| Template  | Abstract program or design     |
| Assembly  | Instantiated runtime structure |
| Execution | Running process                |
| Results   | Output and side effects        |

*You may also retain conventional categories such as:*

- *offensive;*
- *defensive;*
- *sensory;*
- *transformative;*
- *transportive;*
- *communicative;*
- *industrial.*

*However, those are social-use categories, not fundamental theory. They should be explicitly described as practical classifications rather than natural magical divisions.*



# What else should be summarily covered?

The guide does not need to be comprehensive, but several missing sections are foundational enough that later work may otherwise contradict itself.

## 1. Construct interfaces and composition rules

This is the most important missing section.

It should establish:

- what constructs may receive;
- what they may emit;
- how constructs are linked;
- whether links are directional;
- whether linked constructs must be spatially adjacent;
- whether data and mana use the same channels;
- whether connections can be conditional;
- what happens when an output has several recipients;
- what happens when incompatible interfaces are linked.

A small interface taxonomy would be enough:

|Interface|Carries|
|---|---|
|**Flow**|Mana|
|**Control**|Activation, gating, and termination signals|
|**Parameter**|Magnitude, vector, duration, and similar values|
|**Reference**|Reagent or construct identity|
|**State**|Sensed or processed information|
|**Fault**|Error and emergency signals|

You do not need to make these literal wires. They are logical categories of link behaviour.

---

## 2. Construct persistence and lifecycle classes

The five phases describe a lifecycle, but not how long constructs exist.

Cover at least:

- ephemeral constructs;
- reusable dormant constructs;
- sustained active constructs;
- persistent anchored constructs;
- self-reconstructing constructs.

This will prevent later confusion between cantrips, wards, enchantments, and engines.

For example:

|Class|Behaviour|
|---|---|
|**Ephemeral**|Constructed, executed, then dissipated|
|**Dormant**|Remains sealed between activations|
|**Sustained**|Exists only while continuously supplied|
|**Persistent**|Maintains itself through stored or ambient mana|
|**Regenerative**|Repairs or reconstructs damaged components|

---

## 3. Parameter and information model

Your constructs process conditions and parameters, but the guide does not explain what information inside a construct actually is.

A short section should answer:

- Are parameters encoded geometrically?
- Are values continuous or discrete?
- Can a construct store state?
- Can it compare quantities?
- Can it remember prior execution cycles?
- Can it generate a random or indeterminate result?
- Can it represent absent or invalid values?

This is where the system's programmatic character becomes concrete.

You could distinguish:

- **constants**: declared at construction;
- **inputs**: supplied at activation;
- **observations**: acquired during execution;
- **state**: retained between cycles;
- **outputs**: passed to other components or constructs.

---

## 4. Reagent semantics

“Reagent” is central but currently underdefined.

You should summarily establish:

> A reagent is the physical or metaphysical system whose properties are directly operated upon by Attuned mana.

Then clarify whether a reagent may be:

- one object;
- a volume;
- multiple objects;
- a field;
- a relationship;
- a process;
- a living organism;
- another construct.

You should also distinguish:

- **selected reagent**: the thing identified by the construct;
- **bounded reagent**: the exact portion acted upon;
- **affected environment**: things altered only through natural propagation.

For example, in a fire spell:

- the wood is the reagent;
- molecular momentum is increased;
- ignition and flame propagation are natural consequences;
- nearby air may or may not be a secondary reagent.

This distinction is central to maintaining the rule that magic performs primitive operations rather than directly demanding macroscopic outcomes.

---

## 5. Natural consequence doctrine

This principle is present implicitly but should be stated explicitly.

Something like:

> Constructs directly perform only the primitive operations associated with their supplied mana attributes. All unconstrained subsequent effects arise through ordinary physical, biological, or metaphysical processes.

This one paragraph will prevent many later contradictions.

It explains why:

- heating may cause fire;
- pressure may cause rupture;
- Cohesive severing may cause structural collapse;
- Galvanic charge may cause muscle contraction;
- Radiant emission may cause heat indirectly;
- Kinetic changes may produce sound.

It also establishes that a spell designer must predict downstream effects rather than simply declare them.

---

## 6. Magical observability and uncertainty

Since this is a scientific renaissance, define whether constructs and mana can be measured.

You only need a short section establishing that scholars can observe:

- mana concentration;
- type or attribute;
- flow direction;
- potential;
- scaffold geometry;
- component activation;
- waste production.

Also establish limits:

- measurement may disturb Primed mana;
- remote measurement may require Resonant mana;
- Ontic properties may be theory-dependent;
- construct internals may be opaque after sealing.

This creates room for experimental science, instrumentation, and competing models.

---

## 7. Interference and environmental effects

Because mana types repel one another and constructs can disrupt nearby constructs, cover:

- construct proximity;
- attribute contamination;
- field saturation;
- scaffold resonance;
- cross-talk;
- background mana noise;
- shielding.

You do not need equations yet. A summary rule is enough:

> Constructs do not operate in isolation; local mana concentration, nearby attributes, material composition, and active constructs can alter flow, stability, and execution fidelity.

This supports wards, laboratories, magical accidents, and instrument casings.

---

## 8. Casting taxonomy

Your earlier Wild, Reflexive, Structured, and Instrumental material should eventually be brought into this guide, but not necessarily in detail.

See [[00 Casting Theory]]

A summary section should distinguish separate axes:

### Instantiation method

- instinctive;
- reflexive;
- structured;
- formulaic or mechanically copied.

### Hosting substrate

- mental or somatic;
- instrumental;
- architectural;
- environmental.

### Supervision

- manually supervised;
- triggered;
- conditional;
- autonomous;
- adaptive.

This avoids treating Wild, Structured, and Instrumental Casting as mutually exclusive peers.

---

## 9. Conservation and cost principles

You do not need numerical equations, but the guide should state broad invariants.

See [[01 Principles]]

For example:

- mana particles are conserved through permutation;
- mana potential is consumed through expenditure;
- attunement is never perfectly efficient;
- constructs cannot produce an attribute unavailable through their mana supply;
- larger or faster effects require greater mana throughput;
- higher precision requires greater construct complexity or slower execution;
- sustaining non-natural states requires continued expenditure;
- information and control are not free.

These rules will be used constantly in later spell design.
