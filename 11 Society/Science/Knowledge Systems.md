# Knowledge Systems

**Type: Setting reference**

**Status: Current foundation**

Knowledge is a material and political resource. It takes time to acquire, teachers to transmit, instruments and materials to test, records to retain, and institutions to protect or circulate. Its management should therefore follow from incentives rather than from a blanket preference for either secrecy or openness.

## Why Knowledge Fragments

Useful knowledge can remain divided among Houses, guilds, individual masters, governments, religious bodies, merchants, schools, and private collections. Fragmentation may persist because training is expensive, records are difficult to copy or interpret, methods depend on local materials, patrons fund only useful work, or a disclosure would weaken a group's bargaining position.

This does not mean that people are irrational or that every institution rejects evidence. It means that evidence can be hard to compare across incompatible units, terminology, instruments, access rules, and professional interests.

## Questions for Any Claim

- Who paid for the work, and what do they expect in return?
- Who may observe the method, use the instrument, or read the record?
- How is competence recognized: lineage, examination, successful practice, office, reputation, or some combination?
- What makes a result credible locally, and what would make it credible elsewhere?
- Which failures are hidden, forgotten, or too costly to repeat?
- What knowledge is restricted for safety, leverage, custom, or simple scarcity?

## The Protagonist's Reform

The protagonist's challenge is institutional as well as intellectual: gaining access, translating between traditions, calibrating measurements, reproducing results, finding collaborators, and creating ways for findings to outlive a single person or patron. The goal is not indiscriminate publication, but a more reliable and appropriately shared process for producing knowledge.

This system should be expressed locally through [[11 Society/Organizations/Houses|Houses]], [[11 Society/Organizations/Guilds|guilds]], schools, patrons, and the first settlement's actual problems.
