## Historical Composition and Reliability Preferences

These retain their earlier author-confirmed scope and are not a newly approved replacement specification.

- A command such as catching an object requires an existing means of target selection, bounded action, feedback, and stopping
- Most arbitrary alterations to intricate spells cause failure rather than useful innovation, while contextual bugs and unintended effects remain possible
- Public cantrips favour restricted parameters, resilience, and safe failure despite accumulated copying and casting errors
- Design quality, operating conditions, and maintenance have practical consequences
- A simple tool may use bounded control or assist the user's work without reasoning or personhood
- Learning and self-modification require deliberate support; age alone does not create intelligence
- Unmanaged drift usually creates neutral or harmful quirks, while deliberate customization can produce distinctive behaviour without consciousness

## Sharing and Teaching

- Notation makes spells shareable among appropriately prepared recipients, sometimes after adaptation or translation
- Representational limits, specialist conventions, and audience competence constrain reliable transmission
- Public formulas and specialist design descriptions serve different users; knowing a cantrip does not confer its designer's expertise
- The earlier preference to discourage or regulate informal transmission because it accumulates casting dialects needs reconciliation with open sharing; no institutional solution is selected

## Unselected Engineering Practices

The earlier proposals distinguish design notation, rehearsed templates, and running structures. This remains a useful candidate distinction, independent of how the replacement physically realizes a spell. Copying an artefact and sharing an inspectable design need not offer the same capabilities. Ordinary written symbols need not be intrinsically executable.

Candidate practices include component libraries, calibration, diagnostics, translation between notations, checks before admitting substantial mana, and bounded failure. Structural validity would not guarantee correct behaviour. These practices remain proposals rather than mandatory features of the replacement.

## To Develop

- Adaptation across practitioners, tools, languages, and traditions
- Errors detectable before execution and bounded failure for particular operations
- Reliable transmission alongside open sharing, specialized teaching, and personal mastery
