## Retained Practice Direction

Tool familiarity enables finesse. Disuse, replacement, and dependence on personal quirks can make tools unwieldy, while experience with varied tools improves adaptability. The intended distribution of competence remains pyramidal, with many basic practitioners and few exceptional specialists.

The intended renaissance centres on interoperability, standardized methods, and open sharing. Existing practitioners retain meaningful expertise. Personal understanding and tool familiarity coexist with reproducible instruction.

## Historical Learning Preferences

The following retain their earlier author-confirmed scope and are not a newly approved replacement specification.

- Designers understand the components they use and their interactions without having to reconstruct each established component from first principles
- Formula users need relevant fundamentals and instructions rather than the designer's complete understanding
- Learning fundamentals resembles mastering rigorous structured material; expert-designed cantrips can subsequently be accessible and resilient
- The earlier progression runs from ordinary users of roughly five to ten common cantrips, through novices modifying specialized formulas, to designers and specialist experts; the numbers and stages remain revisable
- Physics, chemistry, biology, computing, robotics, and structural engineering provide author-facing analogies for overlapping specializations
- Directly imparting an unexplained mundane skill was disfavoured as a substitute for engineering a spell
