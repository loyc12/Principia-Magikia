## Autonomous Conceptual Webs

**Author-selected development direction:** conceptual webs may operate autonomously and modify their own organization. Such pseudo-minds may exist temporarily without a material body while still requiring a soul-form or equivalent mana-form subject to degradation. They are not established as true minds or conscious persons. The cognitive-substrate constraint must not be used to prohibit this adaptive behaviour merely because it occurs in mana.

**Possible applications, not implemented capabilities:** lichdom, apparently sentient spells or wards, soul magic, mind manipulation, external memory, and externalized thought processes. The author's mana-based graphics-processor analogy describes an auxiliary structure connected to a mind to augment its processing. How it connects, what it can do, and how it differs from a true mind remain open.

Self-modification, persistence, and autonomy require later mechanisms. The separate static/dynamic assembly proposal and its possible coupling to neuronal or galvanic flows are deferred in [[10 Magic System/01 Key Concepts/04 Mana-Forms|Mana-Forms]]. Research into a genuinely cognitive magical substrate remains optional and deferred.

## Death and Identity

Imperfect preservation, reconstruction, and lichdom should be conceivable without trivializing death. Attempts may preserve knowledge without personality, personality without complete memory, or a mourner's incomplete representation of the deceased. Degradation over time is possible. A survivor claimed to be a true mind still requires cognitive support under the substrate constraint above. An autonomous conceptual remnant may instead exhibit pseudo-mind behaviour without that claim. Personhood and continuity should remain philosophically debatable rather than settled by an infallible test.

These aims remain active without requiring a universal noosphere. How conceptual webs persist, degrade, or transfer after loss of the original host remains unresolved.

## Open Implementation Questions

- What persists after a host or power supply is lost, what a replacement host must provide, and what separates an imprint from supported active cognition
