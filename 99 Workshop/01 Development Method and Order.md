# Development Method and Order

**Type: Authorial guide**

**Status: Active**

This guide combines the former Methodology and Worldbuilding notes. It supports author-facing development and audits without establishing a universal theory held by people in the setting.

## Knowledge Layers

For any topic, distinguish these questions:

| Layer | Question |
| --- | --- |
| **Objective reality** | How does it actually work? |
| **Institutional knowledge** | What can particular institutions establish, measure, preserve, or safely do? |
| **Common practice** | What do people successfully do with it every day? |
| **Common belief** | What do they think is happening, and where are they mistaken? |
| **Narrative revelation** | What does the reader learn now, later, or perhaps never? |

## Proportionate Development

Use the order below as a dependency guide. Develop the next constraint needed for the current place, institution, or narrative decision. Do not require a complete world catalogue before the author can write the story.

Keep the distinction between a general institution and a particular local example. The first village can remain a narrative planning note while its location, livelihood, and government are questions. Split those subjects only when independently useful references emerge.

## Development Loop

1. State the question and retain its actual status. Use **Exploring** for new alternatives and **Provisional** for a working proposal. Only an explicit author decision establishes an **Approved baseline** or **Selected baseline**, at the stated scope.
2. Identify physical, material, social, and institutional consequences relevant to that question.
3. Test those consequences against [[00 Core/02 Axioms|Axioms]], [[00 Core/01 Premise|Premise]], and the current topical references.
4. Record answers in their owners. Keep open questions and `VALIDATE` prompts visible, and preserve explicit deferrals.
5. Update [[99 Workshop/00 Current Work Context|Current Work Context]] when work order, next steps, or loose ends change, and before pausing a thread. Reorganization alone does not approve an answer.

## Recommended Build Order

0. Magic system (existing foundation, with unresolved review prompts and deferred development) 
1. Environment & Physical Constraints
2. Resources & Economy
3. Institutions
4. Politics & Power Structures
5. Species
6. Cultures & Local Societies
7. States & Polities
8. Historical Development
9. Current Conflicts & Tensions

---

## 1. Environment & Physical Constraints

Define the world's recurring pressures before its civilizations.

Focus on:

- Geography
- Climate
- Resource distribution
- Mana distribution
- Natural hazards
- Ecology
- Travel & communication

> Civilizations emerge as solutions to environmental problems.

---

## 2. Resources & Economy

Determine what society depends upon.

Define:

- Valuable resources
- Production methods
- Transportation
- Trade
- Labour
- Infrastructure
- Scarcity
- Wealth generation

> Economy determines incentives.

---

## 3. Institutions

Design organizations before countries.

Examples of institution categories:

- Magi Colleges
- Trade Guilds
- Academies
- Document Archives
- Religious organizations
- Bureaucracies
- Military orders
- Financial institutions

For each institution define:

- Purpose
- Resources
- Authority
- Knowledge
- Dependencies
- Internal structure
- Long-term objectives

> Institutions shape civilization more than borders.

---

## 4. Politics & Power

Determine how authority is distributed.

Define:

- Sources of legitimacy
- Distribution of magical power
- Lawmaking
- Administration
- Succession
- Diplomacy
- Political factions

> Politics emerge from competing institutions.

---

## 5. Species

Design species around ecology rather than archetypes.

Define:

- Evolutionary niche
- Physiology
- Sensory differences
- Magical capabilities
- Limitations
- Demographics
- Social tendencies

Species should influence:

- Architecture
- Transportation
- Warfare
- Economy
- Daily life

> Species change civilization through practical consequences.

---

## 6. Cultures & Local Societies

Develop how people live.

Define:

- Values
- Customs
- Education
- Family structures
- Languages
- Religion
- Traditions
- Professions

> Culture emerges from environment, institutions and history.

---

## 7. States & Polities

Build political entities from existing institutions.

Define:

- Territory
- Administrative divisions
- Dominant institutions
- Alliances
- Rivalries
- External relations

> Countries are coalitions of competing interests.

---

## 8. Historical Development

Construct history as accumulated change.

Focus on:

- Technological shifts
- Magical discoveries
- Major migrations
- Arrivals
- Wars
- Schisms
- Institutional evolution

> History explains the present rather than existing for its own sake.

---

## 9. Conflicts & Tensions

Every major element should generate conflict.

For each organization, society, or polity define:

- Goals
- Resources
- Dependencies
- Rivals
- Internal disagreements
- External pressures

> Stable worlds are built from unstable relationships.

---

## General Principles

- Build causes before outcomes.
- Let civilizations emerge from constraints.
- Let institutions shape politics.
- Let species solve ecological niches.
- Let economics drive incentives.
- Let history accumulate naturally.
- Prefer ideological conflicts over purely moral ones.
- Design interactions before isolated factions.
- Every major system should influence several others.
- Every worldbuilding decision should create downstream consequences.
