## Direction Retained for Development

Magic remains compositional. Practitioners assemble learned or understood instructions with inputs, control, and possible autonomous execution. Intent does not supply missing knowledge, procedures, or competence. Personal understanding and experience matter even where established methods are reproducible.

The intended renaissance centres on interoperability, standardized methods, and open sharing. Existing practitioners possess meaningful expertise. Exceptional mastery remains compatible with a pyramidal distribution of competence. Familiarity with tools enables finesse, while disuse, replacement, and dependence on personal quirks can impair their use.

The active model uses mana particles and mana-forms, including naturally occurring soul-forms and artificial spell-forms. It does not import the old declensions, detailed construct architecture, or a particular computational substrate. [[99 Workshop/Magic-System Brainstorms/Souls, Minds, and Conceptual Magic#Unstructured and Structured Casting|The casting model]] owns conceptual webs, chains, duplication, and the optional proposal for inert spell-form libraries; their implementation remains open.

## Historical Preferences Available for Reuse

The following retain their earlier author-confirmed scope. They are useful starting preferences, not a newly approved specification for the replacement.

### Learning and Competence

- Designers understand the components they use and their interactions without having to reconstruct each established component from first principles
- Formula users need relevant fundamentals and instructions rather than the designer's complete understanding
- Learning fundamentals resembles mastering rigorous structured material; expert-designed cantrips can subsequently be accessible and resilient
- The earlier progression runs from ordinary users of roughly five to ten common cantrips, through novices modifying specialized formulas, to designers and specialist experts; the numbers and stages remain revisable
- Physics, chemistry, biology, computing, robotics, and structural engineering provide author-facing analogies for overlapping specializations
- Directly imparting an unexplained mundane skill was disfavoured as a substitute for engineering a spell

### Composition and Reliability

- A command such as catching an object requires an existing means of target selection, bounded action, feedback, and stopping
- Most arbitrary alterations to intricate spells cause failure rather than useful innovation, while contextual bugs and unintended effects remain possible
- Public cantrips favour restricted parameters, resilience, and safe failure despite accumulated copying and casting errors
- Design quality, operating conditions, and maintenance have practical consequences
- A simple tool may use bounded control or assist the user's work without reasoning or personhood
- Learning and self-modification require deliberate support; age alone does not create intelligence
- Unmanaged drift usually creates neutral or harmful quirks, while deliberate customization can produce distinctive behaviour without consciousness

### Sharing and Teaching

- Notation makes spells shareable among appropriately prepared recipients, sometimes after adaptation or translation
- Representational limits, specialist conventions, and audience competence constrain reliable transmission
- Public formulas and specialist design descriptions serve different users; knowing a cantrip does not confer its designer's expertise
- The earlier preference to discourage or regulate informal transmission because it accumulates casting dialects needs reconciliation with open sharing; no institutional solution is selected

## Unselected Engineering Practices

The earlier proposals distinguish design notation, rehearsed templates, and running structures. This remains a useful candidate distinction, independent of how the replacement physically realizes a spell. Copying an artefact and sharing an inspectable design need not offer the same capabilities. Ordinary written symbols need not be intrinsically executable.

Candidate practices include component libraries, calibration, diagnostics, translation between notations, checks before admitting substantial mana, and bounded failure. Structural validity would not guarantee correct behaviour. These practices remain proposals rather than mandatory features of the replacement.

## Questions to Revisit After the Foundations

- Which fundamentals does a formula user actually need, and which can a tool or established component supply
- How much adaptation does sharing require across practitioners, tools, languages, and traditions
- Which errors can be detected before execution, and what constitutes bounded failure for different operations
- How open sharing can coexist with reliable transmission, specialized teaching, and personal mastery
- Which progression stages and everyday capabilities follow from the selected costs and mechanisms

Detailed curricula, cantrip catalogues, institutional policies, and artefact designs can wait until the foundations constrain them.
