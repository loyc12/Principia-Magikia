# The First Village

This is the protagonist's first substantial encounter with organised society. It should be developed as a particular place, not as a representative miniature of the whole Expanse.

## Narrative Function

- It ends survival as the protagonist's only problem without making them immediately secure or understood.
- It provides the first sustained language-learning, friendship, work, and institutional pressures.
- It reveals competent local practice alongside the limits of fragmented knowledge.
- It establishes the contrast from which later towns, cities, and the Great Archives can feel genuinely larger and more connected.

## Site and Livelihood

Decide why people can live here and why they remain. The answer should connect terrain, water, food, building materials, local hazards, trade access, and any nearby magical resource or service.

- What does the settlement produce, repair, exchange, or protect?
- What seasonal or environmental constraint sets its scale?
- Which necessary good must come from elsewhere, and who controls that connection?

## Physical and Social Form

- Population, density, building materials, shared spaces, and accommodation for local species.
- Food, water, heat, waste, lighting, and the visible limits of local infrastructure.
- Social divisions grounded in work, property, magical training, kinship, origin, or institutional membership rather than generic class labels.
- The local language situation: native languages, a shared speech, literacy, interpreters, and what the protagonist first fails to understand.

## Authority and Institutions

Identify the difference between formal government and actual centres of power.

- Which House, guild, council, landholder, temple, official, or practitioner matters locally?
- What useful service or resource makes each claim to authority credible?
- Which knowledge is public, taught through apprenticeship, guarded, or simply unavailable because nobody has compared the relevant practices?
- What rules, obligations, or fears shape the reception of a lone Arrival?

The general roles of [[Houses|Houses]], [[Guilds|Guilds]], and [[Knowledge Systems|knowledge systems]] should constrain these answers without deciding them in advance.

## The Protagonist's First View

Specify both sides of the encounter:

- What seems primitive or strange to the protagonist but has a sound local reason?
- What seems unexpectedly sophisticated in ordinary, non-magical life?
- What local competence does the protagonist initially underestimate?
- What practical need makes their unfamiliar perspective useful, but not instantly decisive?
